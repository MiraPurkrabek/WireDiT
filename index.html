WireDiT

A Vertex-Slot Diffusion Transformer for 3D Wireframe Reconstruction

Jan Škvrna Miroslav Purkrábek Lukáš Neumann

What can street photos tell us about a roof?

Stand outside a house and its highest ridge may be hidden from view. Walk around it, though, and each angle gives a different clue. WireDiT combines those clues to estimate where the roof’s corners lie in 3D and which corners are joined by an edge.

The result is a wireframe, a simple line drawing you can turn in space. It could help estimate roof area for a renovation or identify space for solar panels, even when the original plans are missing.

Research findings

The model works with a sparse 3D point cloud, camera poses, scale-fitted depth maps and semantic labels derived from street-level views. HoHo provides these observations while withholding the original RGB images for privacy.

64

Slots for building corners

WireDiT starts with 64 noisy 3D positions. It moves them toward likely corners, predicts which ones belong to the building, and connects them with edges. A second pass zooms in on the predicted structure.

0.6542

Best score on unseen houses

On the private HoHo test set, WireDiT led on the Hybrid Structure Score (HSS) and vertex F1 (0.7907). Another method scored higher on edge IoU, so recovering connectivity remains a challenge.

+0.1133

HSS from anchoring scene tokens

Tying scene tokens to observed 3D points raised validation HSS from 0.3639 to 0.4772 compared with freely learned tokens. Giving each token a location helps preserve the geometry of the scene.
